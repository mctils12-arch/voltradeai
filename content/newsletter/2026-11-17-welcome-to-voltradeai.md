## Part 1 — The setup

### One country can't make chips. The other can't get them.

In January 2025, the U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security issued the AI Diffusion Rule — a global licensing framework dividing the world into three tiers for advanced semiconductor exports. Tier I (the U.S. plus 18 intelligence-sharing allies) was exempt. Tier II faced caps. Tier III, primarily China, was effectively locked out.

The Dutch government, under U.S. pressure, had already restricted ASML from exporting its most advanced lithography machines — the EUV systems no other company in the world makes — to Chinese customers. As one industry analyst put it: the EUV ban pushes Chinese chip manufacturing back by roughly a decade.

The result: every other chipmaker on earth that wasn't China had a fundamentally different supply/demand picture than before. Add the AI compute buildout layered on top of that — Nvidia ramping Blackwell, hyperscalers buying every HBM module they could source — and you had a setup almost no analyst priced in early enough.

## Part 2 — The numbers

### Three companies. Twelve months. Math most people missed.

Prices below reflect publicly reported returns over roughly the past twelve months. **Not predictions. Not recommendations.** Just what the tape did.

#### ASML — ASML Holding · +97% / 12mo

The Dutch lithography monopoly. ASML is the only company on earth that builds the EUV machines required to make leading-edge logic and HBM memory chips. When the export-control story moved from "headline risk" to "structural moat," the stock followed. 52-week range: roughly $587 to over $1,300.

Worth knowing: ASML's China revenue actually surged in 2023-24 as Chinese customers stockpiled older-generation DUV machines before tighter restrictions took effect. That demand pull-forward is now reversing, and China's share is projected to normalize toward 20–25% of total sales. The story going forward isn't China — it's whether AI demand stays high enough to absorb that gap.

#### MU — Micron Technology · +330% / 12mo

Memory was supposed to be a low-margin commodity. Then AI happened. Micron's HBM3E modules ship inside Nvidia's Blackwell GPUs, and demand for high-bandwidth memory turned out to be more constrained than supply for nearly the entire twelve-month period.

The data point worth holding: in Micron's FY2025, data center went from 35% of revenue to 56%. HBM hit an annualized run rate of $8 billion in a single quarter. By early 2026, Micron's HBM4 capacity for the year was reportedly sold out before it was made. Memory stopped being a commodity, at least for now.

#### SNDK — Sandisk Corp. · +3,000%+ / 12mo

The most extraordinary chart of the three. Spun off from Western Digital in February 2025, SanDisk traded under $30 in the spin-off-panic weeks. Twelve months later: over $850. AI data centers don't just need HBM — they need NAND flash, lots of it, and the supply/demand was even more dislocated than memory.

Why it happened: contract NAND prices rose 85–90% quarter-over-quarter in multiple quarters. Sandisk's gross margins went from 26% to a guided 65–67% in a year. The NAND shortage is projected by some industry trackers to persist through 2028. A spin-off priced for a cyclical bottom ran into a structural shortage.
