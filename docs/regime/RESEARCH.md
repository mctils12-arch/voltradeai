# RESEARCH — what the evidence says about market regimes, volatility and breadth (Stage A, Part 1)

Written 2026-10-27 for the AlphaDesk Market Regime & Volatility Engine program. Companion files: `BASELINE.md` (frozen reference), `AUDIT.md` (what the code does today; its §11 cites the section numbers below), `FINDINGS.md` (verdict and plan).

## 0. How this was produced and how to read it

- Four parallel literature reviews were run and then reconciled by hand: regime-switching and statistical models (R1), trend/breadth/dip-buying/small-caps (R2), volatility/options/credit (R3), practitioner research/ML/transition logic (R4). Every source below carries a verification tag: **[FULL]** = the paper or page was opened and read (local text copies kept under the session scratchpad `pdfs/`), **[ABSTRACT]** = only the abstract or bibliographic record was opened, **[SEARCH-ONLY]** = numbers come from search snippets and are *not* used for any rule, **[UNVERIFIED]** = could not be opened at all. Rules in this document rest only on [FULL] or [ABSTRACT] sources unless a line says otherwise.
- Every method is reported on the same fields: sources, evidence quality (**strong / mixed / weak**), out-of-sample results, behaviour in the five test episodes (2008, 2015–16, late 2018, 2020, 2022), known failure modes, whether it **leads or reacts**, implementation cost, data required and free-ness, and fit for this repo. "n.d." means not documented in any opened source.
- Section 5 is our own index-level replication on the repo's free data (pre-registered priors, then results). It exists because almost no opened source gives per-episode numbers for the simple rules, and because the only way to know what a rule does on *this* data path is to run it here.
- Data constraint that shapes everything: the repo has free daily bars (Alpaca first, Yahoo fallback, `backtest_v2.fetch_bars`) back to 2007-08 for SPY/QQQ/IWM/RSP/HYG/IEF/^VIX/^VIX3M, ^VIX9D from 2011, an options-chain archive of ~80 days, no breadth feed, no market-cap field, and **no delisted names anywhere** (AUDIT.md §4–§5). Anything that needs point-in-time constituents, intraday data, open interest or VIX futures is marked as such.

Standing caveat from every strong source: **no regime signal in this review leads a drawdown by days.** The honest claims are (a) persistence — today's state tends to continue — and (b) conditioning — some strategies have different expectancy by state. Anything sold as "early warning" is either coincident, in-sample, or both.

## 1. Regime-switching and statistical regime models

### 1.1 Markov-switching models (Hamilton 1989; Ang & Bekaert 2002/2004; Guidolin & Timmermann 2007)
- **Sources:** Hamilton 2016 NBER w21863 [FULL]; Ang & Bekaert 2002 (Columbia PDF) [FULL]; Ang & Bekaert 2004 (NBER w10080) [FULL]; Guidolin & Timmermann 2005 WP of the 2007 JEDC paper [FULL]; Ang & Timmermann 2011 NBER w17182 [FULL]; Hamilton 1989 [ABSTRACT].
- **Evidence quality:** mixed. The statistical finding is strong and universal: monthly equity data cannot reject equal *means* across regimes but overwhelmingly reject equal *variances*; the high-variance regime has low average return (Ang & Timmermann). The allocation results are sample-specific: Ang & Bekaert 2002 report a 1985–2000 Sharpe of 1.07 vs 0.90 with no costs; Guidolin & Timmermann's four-state model wins pseudo-OOS only at ≥12-month horizons.
- **Out-of-sample:** thin and monthly; the four-state model is explicitly admitted to have "benefited from full-sample information".
- **Crisis behaviour:** 2008 inside some samples as a high-variance state; 2015–16, late 2018, 2020, 2022: n.d.
- **Failure modes:** more than two states overfit; smoothed probabilities peek at the future (Hamilton 2016); regimes are identified with a lag; label switching across refits.
- **Leads or reacts:** reacts.
- **Cost / data:** statsmodels/hmmlearn, monthly index data (free).
- **Fit:** a design template only — the lesson is "regimes are volatility regimes, two states, monthly for the slow leg". Not a daily engine.

### 1.2 Hidden Markov models in practice (Kritzman–Page–Turkington 2012; Two Sigma 2021; Nystrup et al. 2014–2018; Bulla et al. 2011)
- **Sources:** Bulla, Mergner, Bulla, Sesboüé & Chesneau 2011 (MPRA 21154) [FULL]; Nystrup 2014 MSc and 2017/18 PhD theses [FULL]; Nystrup–Madsen–Lindström 2018 QF manuscript [FULL]; Two Sigma 2021 GMM note [FULL]; Kritzman–Page–Turkington 2012 [ABSTRACT, details via Nystrup 2014]; Jasra–Holmes–Stephens 2005 label switching [ABSTRACT].
- **Evidence quality:** mixed-to-strong for Bulla (five daily indices, rolling 2,000-day refit, Viterbi decoding, 6-day median filter, 10 bp costs); weak for the descriptive GMM "regime maps" (Two Sigma: "not predictive").
- **Out-of-sample (Bulla, verified):** volatility −41% on average; excess return over the index from +18.5 bp/yr (S&P 500) to +201.6 bp/yr (Nikkei); strategy Sharpe 0.437–0.646 vs an index average of 0.342; the 6-day majority filter cuts state changes 50–65% (DAX 188→84, DJIA 190→60, NASDAQ 59→31). The in-sample, no-cost S&P Sharpe of 0.91 vs 0.37 shows the in-sample picture flatters by ~3×. Nystrup's thesis adds the sharpest caveat: on a single index, remove the 2008 loss and the switching strategy "would not outperform the index in terms of absolute return"; it lagged the 2009 rebound and underperformed static weights in 2015.
- **Crisis behaviour:** Bulla's sample ends before 2008 (n.d. for all five episodes); Nystrup 2018 covers 1997–2015 and reports the 2015 underperformance.
- **Failure modes (documented):** label switching across refits; ~10% misclassification at the live end of the sequence (hence Viterbi, not smoothing); unfiltered HMMs over-switch (~190 switches per 5,800 days); parameter instability over decades; misspecification collapses the model to a mixture with no temporal content (Nystrup–Lindström–Madsen 2020); `hmmlearn`'s posterior method is forward–backward, i.e. *smoothed* — a live signal must use the forward recursion or an expanding-window refit ending at t.
- **Leads or reacts:** reacts, plus ≥6 days of filter delay.
- **Cost / data:** ~80 lines with hmmlearn; daily closes (free).
- **Fit:** the HMM is the *benchmark to beat* in any comparison, not the engine. Its one transferable element is the dwell mechanism (majority filter), which §4.3 implements directly. The repo's `markov_regime.py` is not this: it is a hard-coded order-2 matrix fitted once on 2016–2026 and never refit (AUDIT.md §2, §9).

### 1.3 Statistical jump models (Bemporad et al. 2018; Nystrup–Lindström–Madsen 2020; Shu–Yu–Mulvey 2024)
- **Sources:** Bemporad–Breschi–Piga–Boyd 2018 arXiv [FULL]; Nystrup–Lindström–Madsen 2020 ESWA manuscript [FULL]; Nystrup–Kolm–Lindström 2021 ESWA [FULL]; Shu–Yu–Mulvey 2024 JAM (arXiv 2402.05272v3) [FULL]; the two 2024 follow-ups (asset-specific, factor) [FULL]; `jumpmodels` README [FULL]; Aydınhan–Kolm–Mulvey–Shu 2024 continuous JM [ABSTRACT].
- **Evidence quality:** strong within one research group (Mulvey/Shu with Kolm/Nystrup/Lindström); no independent replication found; λ tuned on a single validation window.
- **Out-of-sample (verified, Shu–Yu–Mulvey Table 4, OOS 1990–2023, 10 bp, 1-day delay):** S&P 500 buy-and-hold CAGR 10.2% / Sharpe 0.48 / max DD −55.2%; HMM 8.5% / 0.54 / −28.9% at 141% turnover; jump model 11.2% / 0.68 / −26.6% at 44% turnover. DAX and Nikkei show the same ordering. Robust to 1/5/10-day execution delays (S&P JM Sharpe 0.68/0.71/0.70 vs HMM 0.54/0.55/0.51). Flip rate: λ = 35/70/150 gives 0.8/0.5/0.4 state changes per year; online S&P 1990–2023 ≈ 0.9/yr. Linear-in-probability allocation ≈ 0/1 switching (graduation does not hurt).
- **Crisis behaviour:** 2008 "captured"; 2015–16 the authors admit a lag; late 2018 n.d.; 2020 "roughly half a month of latency", avoided ≈20% of drawdown but "missed part of the rebound"; 2022 captured as a bear regime.
- **Failure modes (stated by the authors):** latency "inherent"; sharp two-way swings misread; "they do not forecast next-day regimes"; the all-or-nothing switch "may be too extreme"; live inference produces ~2× the in-sample switch count; the λ grid is a multiple-hypothesis search.
- **Leads or reacts:** reacts (~2 weeks in 2020).
- **Cost / data:** `pip install jumpmodels` or ~200 lines; one daily total-return series per asset plus T-bill (free).
- **Fit:** the best-evidenced *model-based* classifier in the review — but note what it is: a persistence-penalised clustering of three volatility/Sortino features, i.e. a dwell mechanism on a realized-vol state. §5 shows the repo's objective is served by the same two ingredients (realized vol, slow trend) without the model; the jump model remains the right *benchmark* if a model-based engine is ever proposed, and it must be re-run on IWM before any small-cap claim.

### 1.4 Change-point methods (Adams & MacKay 2007; Nystrup et al. 2016; Knoblauch 2018)
- **Sources:** Adams & MacKay 2007 arXiv [FULL]; Knoblauch–Jewson–Damoulas 2018 arXiv [FULL, first half]; Nystrup–Hansen–Madsen–Lindström 2016 JAM [ABSTRACT]; an ACM 2025 BOCPD-in-finance paper [UNVERIFIED, 403].
- **Evidence quality:** the method is strong; the finance evidence is thin or paywalled. Knoblauch shows standard BOCPD gives >90% false discoveries on an outlier-laden benchmark; robust (β-divergence) variants fix it but have no published trading record.
- **Out-of-sample:** Nystrup 2016 claims Sharpe and drawdown improvement vs static (numbers unverified).
- **Crisis behaviour:** n.d.
- **Failure modes:** fat tails trigger false alarms; hazard-rate and prior choices; a change alarm says "something changed", not what.
- **Leads or reacts:** reacts.
- **Cost / data:** ~100 lines NumPy; daily returns or VIX (free).
- **Fit:** a dashboard alarm at most; not a trading input until it passes ladder gate 2 with a robust variant.

### 1.5 Clustering return distributions (Horvath–Issa–Muguruza 2021; GMM / k-means)
- **Sources:** Horvath–Issa–Muguruza 2021 arXiv [FULL]; Two Sigma 2021 [FULL]; Akioyamen–Tang–Hussien 2020 [ABSTRACT].
- **Evidence quality:** weak. A posteriori labels, no OOS, no trading test; synthetic accuracy not better than moment k-means on geometric Brownian motion; Two Sigma's own statement that the labels are "not predictive".
- **Fit:** not an engine. k-means is the λ = 0 ablation of §1.3 and nothing more.

### 1.6 What §1 settles
1. Equity regimes are **volatility regimes**; target realized/downside volatility, not direction.
2. **Two states** are enough on daily index data; more states hurt out of sample.
3. Switching an index to cash in the high-volatility state cuts volatility ~30–40% and max drawdown by about half, with a small-positive-to-neutral CAGR effect after costs and delay. This is the prior for *any* regime overlay: a drawdown tool, not an alpha source.
4. **Turnover control is what makes it survive** (jump penalty, majority filter, adaptive estimator); every successful paper has one.
5. Detection lags days to weeks and **does not forecast** switches; persistence is the exploitable property.
6. Backtests must use filtered/online inference with strictly past parameters; a hard-coded transition matrix fitted on the test period (the repo's `markov_regime.py`) is look-ahead by construction.

## 2. Trend, dip-buying, breadth, falling knives, small caps

### 2.1 Index trend filters (Faber; Moskowitz–Ooi–Pedersen; Hurst–Ooi–Pedersen; Zakamulin; Kaminski; the 200-day specifically)
- **Sources:** Faber 2007/2013 (Cambria PDF) [FULL]; Moskowitz–Ooi–Pedersen 2012 JFE [FULL]; Hurst–Ooi–Pedersen 2017 "A Century of Evidence" [FULL]; Zakamulin 2015 SSRN 2585056 (mirror PDF) [FULL] and 2014 JAM [ABSTRACT]; Kaminski & Zhao 2025 (AlphaSimplex) [FULL]; Graham Capital trend primer [FULL]; Hill/StockCharts 2019 200-day anatomy [FULL]; Carlson 2018 [FULL]; Newfound 2018–2020 pieces [FULL]; Sullivan–Timmermann–White 1999 [FULL]; Brock–Lakonishok–LeBaron 1992 [UNVERIFIED at source, secondary only].
- **Evidence quality:** strong for the existence of a long-horizon trend effect (MOP: 12-month TSMOM positive for all 58 contracts, t = 6.61 pooled; equity-index-only 12/1 t = 3.77 while 1/1 is t = 1.05 — short lookbacks on equity indices are the weakest cell); strong *skeptical* evidence from Zakamulin (1870–2010 OOS: most timing rules beat the market risk-adjusted, a few significantly, but the probability of outperforming over 5–10-year horizons is "barely above 50%", and the equal-weighted momentum rule beats exotic moving averages).
- **Out-of-sample:** Faber labels post-2005 as OOS: five-asset timing beat buy-and-hold by >2 pp/yr 2006–12 with lower drawdown, outperforming in 3 of 7 years; S&P 500 1901–2012 timing CAGR 10.18% vs 9.32% B&H, invested ~70% of the time, <1 round trip/yr, max drawdown 42% vs 84%; SMA lengths 3–12 months "all seem to work similarly". Hurst et al.: trend positive in every decade 1880–2016 across asset classes.
- **Crisis behaviour:** Kaminski's SG Trend Index table against S&P 500 TR drawdowns: GFC −55.25% → trend **+32.11%**; 2000–02 −47.41% → **+62.26%**; 2022 −24.49% → **+35.64%**; but COVID (23 days, −33.79%) → **−2.44%**, Q4 2018 (67 days, −19.36%) → **−3.32%**, 2011 (−18.64%) → **−4.98%**. The boundary between a crisis trend pays in and a correction it loses in is "somewhere around 15%". (The SG index is multi-asset; its 2022 gain came largely from short bonds/long commodities, which an equity-only gate cannot earn.) The 200-day on daily closes: ~180 crosses in 25 years (≈7/yr), 90 completed long signals of which 20 won (+13.7% avg) and 70 lost (−1.3% avg); a 5-day SMA cross cuts signals to 34; Newfound: four trend formulas differ by ~350 bp/yr long-run, six lookbacks deviate by >500 bp over rolling 5-year windows, and rebalance-week choice alone turned $1 into $4,139 vs $6,797 ("timing luck").
- **Failure modes:** fast crashes and sub-15% corrections; sideways tapes (2015–16, 2022 with ~12 daily 200-day crosses); specification and rebalance-day luck; lag of up to a month on the monthly form.
- **Leads or reacts:** reacts, by construction.
- **Cost / data:** trivial; daily or monthly index closes (free).
- **Fit:** the slow gate. Use it as an *environment classifier* (Carlson) with de-whipsawing (band or smoothed cross), diversified lookbacks and tranched evaluation days, on the index the universe actually tracks (IWM/RSP for small/mid), and expect drawdown control in long bears, loss in V-shaped corrections, and nothing in most years. Never a single lookback.

### 2.2 Short-term reversal, dip-buying, the 52-week high, and the panic state (Jegadeesh; Lehmann; Nagel; Da–Liu–Schaumburg; Connors re-test; George & Hwang; Byun & Jeon; Daniel & Moskowitz; Barroso & Santa-Clara; Cooper–Gutierrez–Hameed)
- **Sources:** Nagel 2012 (NBER w17653) [FULL]; Da–Liu–Schaumburg 2011 (FRBNY SR 513) [FULL]; Jegadeesh 1990 and Lehmann 1990 [ABSTRACT]; Empirical Lab Connors RSI(2) re-test on SPY 1993–2026 [FULL]; George & Hwang 2004 JF (author PDF) [FULL]; Byun & Jeon 2023 FAJ [ABSTRACT]; Daniel & Moskowitz 2016 JFE (open access) [FULL]; Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015 [ABSTRACT, method confirmed via a GARP replication FULL]; Cooper–Gutierrez–Hameed 2004 [SEARCH-ONLY for numbers; the qualitative result is verified inside Daniel & Moskowitz's text].
- **Evidence quality:** strong that one-week/one-month reversal exists and is a *long-short liquidity-provision* return concentrated in illiquid names and high-VIX states (Nagel: reversal returns "highly predictable with the VIX", no losing three-month period 1998–2010 before costs; Da et al.: the long side is "liquidity shocks"); weak for the long-only "buy the dip" rule (one single-ETF re-test). Strong for the 52-week-high effect (George & Hwang: 52-week-high spread 0.45%/month, 1.23% ex-January, dominating 6-6 momentum in nested sorts; Byun & Jeon: momentum crashes are driven by stocks far from their highs). Strong for the panic state (Daniel & Moskowitz: momentum crashes occur when the past-24-month market return is negative AND ex-ante variance is high; WML beta falls by 0.518, t = −28.4, in panic states; the loser decile ripped +163% Mar–May 2009).
- **Out-of-sample:** Connors RSI(2) on SPY (entry RSI(2) < 10 and close > 200-day SMA, exit close > 5-day SMA): pre-2009 Sharpe 0.97 → post-2009 0.68; full period 5.0%/yr, Sharpe 0.81, max DD 14.8% vs SPY 10.8%/yr, 0.65, 55.2%; **in 2001, 2002 and 2008 the rule made zero trades because SPY was below its 200-day — the filter, not the dip-buy, avoided the bears.** Barroso & Santa-Clara: scaling momentum to a 12% vol target on trailing 6-month realized variance "virtually eliminates crashes and nearly doubles the Sharpe ratio" (GARP replication 0.52 → 1.08; often implies leverage > 1, which the repo must cap).
- **Crisis behaviour:** 2007–09: market-neutral reversal highly profitable (Nagel), the long-only 200-day-filtered dip-buyer was in cash; 2009 is the defining momentum crash; the worst Connors trade in 33 years was Jul–Aug 2011 (−10.2%); 2015–16, late 2018, 2020, 2022: n.d.
- **Failure modes:** the academic reversal premium is not evidence for net-long dip-buying in downtrends; it decays post-publication; single-trade tail losses dominate its drawdown; the 52-week-high screen crowds into leaders and has a January reversal.
- **Leads or reacts:** the panic-state indicator is ex ante (lagged 24-month return + lagged 126-day variance) and *conditions*; everything else reacts.
- **Cost / data:** trivial; daily bars and ^VIX (free).
- **Fit:** this is the direct refutation of the repo's current selection path (AUDIT.md §6): (a) long-only dip entries only when the gating index is above its 200-day; (b) prefer names ≥75% of their 252-day high, flag <50% as momentum-crash fuel rather than value; (c) define PANIC = SPY 24-month return < 0 AND 126-day realized variance in its top quintile, and in PANIC suppress momentum longs and never short/avoid losers on momentum grounds; (d) reversal in bear states only as a market-neutral book, which this bot does not run — so in practice, no dip-buying in bear states at all.

### 2.3 Breadth and dispersion (percent above moving averages; advance–decline; new highs–lows; thrusts; 90% days; RSP/SPY; Zaremba; dispersion and implied correlation)
- **Sources:** Grimes "The Breadth Myth" [FULL]; SentimenTrader 2018 and 2026 breadth-divergence posts [FULL]; Navera Research part 1 [FULL, partly paywalled]; LPL via Investing.com [FULL]; SentimenTrader A/D-line breakout 2023 [FULL]; StockCharts Hindenburg Omen page [FULL]; Carson/Detrick 2023 reporting Deemer and NDR thrust tallies [FULL]; Desmond 2002 Lowry's 90% days (Dow Award PDF) [FULL]; thetrading.tools equal-weight vs cap-weight (CRSP since 1926) [FULL]; Goldman 2020 via CIO magazine [FULL, secondary]; Zaremba et al. 2021 [ABSTRACT/record; numbers SEARCH-ONLY]; Stivers & Sun 2010 [ABSTRACT]; Driessen–Maenhout–Vilkov 2013 Netspar DP [FULL]; Cboe DSPX/COR3M definition pages [FULL]; Zweig thrust statistics [UNVERIFIED].
- **Evidence quality:** weak-to-mixed as a *leading* indicator. Grimes (S&P 500, ~35 years, nine measures): no significant difference in forward-quarter returns after strong vs weak-breadth highs; the frequency of a ≥10% decline within three months after a weak-breadth high was 5–6% vs 13–14% after *strong*-breadth highs (wrong sign), and the effect flipped around 2010. SentimenTrader (300+ divergences since 1928): "not consistently successful sell signals"; post-2008 the S&P was higher a year later 92% of the time. Mixed-but-consistent for the bullish thrusts: Deemer's 10-day A/D > 1.97 was followed by a higher S&P a year later 23 of 24 times since 1949 (avg +20.7%); NDR's > 2.10, 14 of 14 at six months. Desmond's 90% days: descriptive over 69 years, with the author's own documented failure (Aug 1990). RSP/SPY narrowness: after the narrowest tenth of months since 1929, the *next-year* median return was not worse (16.1% vs 13.3%) — equal weight beats cap weight only over five years (4.1 pp/yr, 95% of non-overlapping periods). Implied correlation (DMV): R² 14% at six months, in-sample 1996–2012, not free.
- **Out-of-sample:** none for any breadth timing rule.
- **Crisis behaviour:** Goldman 2020: median stock −28% vs index −17% in April 2020 (narrow leadership masks the median stock); otherwise n.d.
- **Failure modes:** sign flip after 2010; small samples (6–8 weak-breadth highs per measure); the Hindenburg Omen's ~80% false-positive rate (unsourced, StockCharts' own description); exchange-wide breadth data not in the repo's feeds.
- **Leads or reacts:** coincident at best; thrusts lead *recoveries* by 6–12 months (claimed, practitioner).
- **Cost / data:** cheap to compute from the repo's own universe bars (free); NYSE-wide series not available.
- **Fit:** compute it, surface it, but **never sell on it**. Two defensible uses: (1) a "do not add net exposure" caution flag when the gating index is within 3% of its 52-week high and < 50% of the universe is above its 200-day; (2) a re-risk accelerator after a bear (10-day A/D > 2.0 on the universe) that lets exposure rise ahead of the slow gate's re-cross — each needs its own gate-2 test on the repo's universe before it gates anything. RSP/SPY and IWM/SPY tell you *which index to gate on* and how to tilt the sleeve, not when to sell.

### 2.4 Narrow leadership and falling knives (Brandes Institute 2004; Minervini / O'Neil templates)
- **Sources:** Brandes "Falling Knives Around the World" via Allan Gray's contrarian guide [FULL, secondary]; Minervini template as listed by EasySwing and an MQL5 implementation [FULL, books not opened]; O'Neil RS rules via Macro-Ops and a Portfolio123 CAN SLIM test [FULL]; "RS line new high before price", "reclaim the 10/20-day", "no new low in N days", "sector ETF above its 50-day" [no study of any kind found].
- **Evidence quality:** weak. Brandes: US 1980–2003, a "knife" = −60% in 12 months; knives returned 11.2%/yr over the next three years vs 4.6% for the S&P even counting bankruptcies — a mean driven by a few big winners over a three-year hold (the median-underperformance caveat appears only in snippets). The templates have no OOS test; a full CAN SLIM screen underperformed in one 15-year test.
- **Fit:** the only quantified knife study supports *long-horizon, diversified* contrarian holding with heavy right skew — the opposite of a daily dip-buy with a 6% stop. Adopt from the templates only the academically backed subset: price ≥ 75% of the 252-day high (George & Hwang), price above a rising 200-day (trend literature), 3–12-month return vs the gating index in the top tercile (cross-sectional momentum, panic-conditioned). "Reclaim the 10/20-day", "no new 20-day low" and "sector above its 50-day" are untested lore — log them counterfactually, never ship them as evidence-based.

### 2.5 Small and mid caps versus large caps (Perez-Quiros & Timmermann 2000; Hahn & Lee 2006; Chan & Chen 1991; drawdown history; bucket thresholds)
- **Sources:** Perez-Quiros & Timmermann 2000 JF (LSE DP 335) [FULL]; Hahn & Lee 2006 JFQA [ABSTRACT]; Chan & Chen 1991 JF [ABSTRACT]; Marquette Associates 2020 bear-market tables [FULL]; Infrastructure Capital 2025 Russell-vs-S&P risk note [FULL]; Russell 2000 2022 trough [SEARCH-ONLY]; Calamos and Royce post-bear statistics [UNVERIFIED].
- **Evidence quality:** strong for the mechanism (small firms carry the highest risk asymmetry across recession/expansion states and the highest sensitivity to credit conditions — default premium, money growth, rates; Hahn & Lee: small-stock portfolios load more on changes in the default spread; Chan & Chen: small-cap indices are full of "marginal firms" with high leverage); mixed for the descriptive drawdown tables; weak (my inference) for bucket-specific thresholds — no study tests them.
- **Out-of-sample:** PQT run an expanding-window allocation from 1976 (numbers not extracted).
- **Crisis behaviour (Marquette, close-to-close):** Russell 2000 bears: 2007–09 −59.9% (416 days to trough, 539 to recover) vs S&P −56.8% (355 / 1,021); 2011 −29.6%, 2015–16 −26.4%, late 2018 −20% by 17 Dec 2018 — three Russell bears in years when the S&P never closed −20%; March 2020 the Russell was −36.4% from its *August 2018* high; 10-year max DD to Aug 2025 41.75% (Russell) vs 33.79% (S&P), 5% monthly VaR 9.26% vs 6.63%. 2022: S&P −24.49% TR; Russell "about a third" [SEARCH-ONLY].
- **Failure modes:** a single SPY gate keeps a small-cap sleeve "on" through small-cap bears (2015–16) and flips off late; a single vol threshold calibrated on SPY is hit by IWM far more often (a −20% event is roughly twice as frequent in the Russell 2000).
- **Leads or reacts:** the credit variables lead small-firm expected returns at monthly frequency; small caps have peaked earlier (2007, 2018) and bottomed with the market.
- **Cost / data:** IWM, SPY, RSP, HYG, IEF daily (free); FRED series (free, HY OAS now licence-restricted and truncated to three years — §3.5).
- **Fit:** the one place the literature says buckets differ: gate the small/mid sleeve on IWM (or RSP), same functional form, bands scaled to each index's own 20-day σ rather than a fixed 1%, and apply the credit axis only to the small/mid sleeve. Hypothesis for gates 2–3, not a finding.

## 3. Volatility, options and credit

### 3.1 Volatility targeting and volatility-managed portfolios (Moreira & Muir; Cederburg et al.; Liu–Tang–Zhou; Barroso & Detzel; Harvey et al. / Man AHL; Bongaerts–Kang–van Dijk; DeMiguel et al.)
- **Sources:** Moreira & Muir 2017 JF (author PDF) [FULL]; Harvey, Hoyle, Korgaonkar, Rattray, Sargaison & van Hemert 2018 JPM (Duke PDF) [FULL]; Bongaerts–Kang–van Dijk 2020 FAJ (Erasmus repository) [FULL]; Barroso & Detzel 2021 (2018 draft) [FULL]; DeMiguel–Martín-Utrera–Uppal 2022 WP [FULL]; Cederburg–O'Doherty–Wang–Yan 2020 [ABSTRACT, also read through the Visser thesis]; Liu–Tang–Zhou 2019 [ABSTRACT]; Mylnikov 2021 [SEARCH-ONLY].
- **Evidence quality:** mixed for the original alpha claim (in-sample scaling constant; the three later papers show the real-time, after-cost, *factor-level* gains largely vanish and that levering up in calm states produced 68–93% real-time drawdowns); strong for the descriptive facts (Harvey et al., 60+ assets, US equities 1927–2017: Sharpe 0.40 → 0.48–0.51 with vol scaling, "not very sensitive" to the estimator, left tail and vol-of-vol reliably reduced; exception 1957–1987 where the equity Sharpe did not improve; the gain holds only for equities and credit); strong-to-mixed for the conditional version (Bongaerts: act only when last month's realized vol is in its top quintile — ten markets, Sharpe +0.07, max DD −6.6% on average, US +0.16; significant only in the US and Hong Kong).
- **Out-of-sample:** Bongaerts is the explicit real-time, look-ahead-fixed test; Cederburg's real-time factor test is negative.
- **Crisis behaviour:** Moreira & Muir: in 2008 the strategy "cashed out almost completely and returned to the market only as the spike in volatility receded" (recession beta 0.32 vs 0.83); Man 2020 (via CXO [FULL page]): vol targeting cut exposure "ahead of the highest volatility in March 2020"; 2015–16, late 2018, 2022: n.d. in the papers (see §5 for our own numbers).
- **Failure modes:** sells after losses and re-enters after vol recedes, so it structurally misses V-shaped rebounds (2020); turnover "often more than 200%/yr" in the conventional form; no gain for bonds/FX/commodities; it is a time-series-momentum bet in disguise and inherits whipsaw.
- **Leads or reacts:** reacts (exploits volatility persistence).
- **Cost / data:** trivial; daily returns (free). Costs: ≈0.07%/yr at a 10-day half-life on a 10%-vol strategy (Harvey).
- **Fit:** the implementation every paper agrees on: **de-risk only, cap 1.0, EWMA with a ~20-day half-life (or previous-month RV), act in the extreme state, fixed ex-ante target from ≥10 years of prior data.** For the equity sleeve it is a continuous multiplier; for the CSP sleeve it governs position count and notional, never on/off.

### 3.2 Variance risk premium (Bollerslev–Tauchen–Zhou 2009; Carr & Wu 2009; Bekaert & Hoerova 2014; Israelov & Nielsen; Bondarenko)
- **Sources:** BTZ 2009 RFS (Duke PDF) [FULL]; Carr & Wu 2009 (2004 draft) [FULL]; Bekaert & Hoerova 2014 [ABSTRACT]; Israelov & Nielsen 2015 "Still Not Cheap" (AQR) [FULL]; Bondarenko 2019 (Cboe) [FULL].
- **Evidence quality:** mixed-to-strong. BTZ: VRP (VIX² minus intraday realized variance) predicts S&P returns most strongly at the quarterly horizon (adj. R² 6.82%, t = 2.86), dominating P/E, the default spread and cay; the authors stress the intraday realized measure. Israelov & Nielsen: ex-ante VRP (VIX − subsequent realized) averaged +3.4 vol points, positive 88% of months; Bondarenko: average 4.2, 2008 the only negative year (VIX 32.7 vs realized 35.2).
- **Out-of-sample:** in-sample (1990–2007); Johnson's draft reports continued predictability 2008–10.
- **Crisis behaviour:** VRP turns negative exactly in the crash months (2008); otherwise n.d.
- **Failure modes:** sign flips when realized explodes above implied; the 1-month horizon is weak; daily-bar realized variance is noisier than the intraday measure.
- **Leads or reacts:** leads *returns* at 1–3 months, but the high reading appears after a vol spike — it leads the recovery, not the drawdown.
- **Cost / data:** trivial; ^VIX and SPY daily (free). The repo's chain archive adds per-name ATM IV (`chain_iv30`, ~80 days).
- **Fit:** `VIX − RV(21d, annualized)` as the "is premium selling currently paid?" gauge: expect +3 to +4 points on average; a negative reading is the "do not add short vol" state. Not a crash predictor.

### 3.3 VIX term structure, VIX level/percentile/speed, VVIX (Simon & Campasano; Johnson; Fassas & Hourvouliades; CXO; IVolatility; Cboe VVIX; Huang et al.)
- **Sources:** Simon & Campasano 2014 JoD (EFMA 2013 WP) [FULL]; Johnson 2017 (2011 draft) [ABSTRACT + FULL draft]; Fassas & Hourvouliades 2019 [ABSTRACT]; CXO VIX-signaled strategy and monthly VIX predictive-power pages [FULL pages]; IVolatility 2021–26 study [FULL page]; Cboe VVIX note 2012 [FULL]; Huang–Schlag–Shaliastovich–Thimme 2019 [ABSTRACT]; Cboe term-structure page [FULL page, no rule given]; Bansal–Stivers via Quantpedia [ABSTRACT-level].
- **Evidence quality:** mixed. Simon & Campasano's regime table is the useful verified piece: with VIX < 20 the front/second VIX futures bases are in contango 78%/91% of the time; at VIX 40–50 only 46%/32%; the basis "does not have significant forecast power for the change in the spot VIX" — backwardation is a *state*, not a forecast that vol will fall. VIX level: CXO/Cipollini–Manzini (1990–2007): no useful linear relation between VIX level and 3-month returns, but in-sample returns *increase* with the VIX class; CXO 1993–2011: R² of next-month SPY on VIX close 0.00; IVolatility (SPY 2021–26): +3.78% mean 20-day return when IVX30 is 25–35 vs +0.98% all days (not significant on non-overlapping windows). VVIX: 60–145 around 86 (2006–12), weakly related to VIX except at high VIX; predicts delta-hedged option payoffs, says nothing about equities.
- **Out-of-sample:** none for any VIX-level rule; Simon & Campasano's futures strategies are OOS-robust but trade VIX futures, not equities.
- **Crisis behaviour:** 2008 inside samples (inversion at high VIX, VVIX peak Oct 2008); the "mean reversion of fear" works on average and failed in the one case that matters (Oct 2008 high VIX was followed by lower lows); others n.d.
- **Failure modes:** every VIX-family variable is coincident with or lags the price move — the curve inverts *because* spot spiked; high VIX contains both the top of a crash and the middle of it with no level that separates them; the VVIX floor shifted in 2010.
- **Leads or reacts:** reacts, all of them.
- **Cost / data:** trivial; ^VIX, ^VIX3M (2007–), ^VIX9D (2011–), ^VVIX (free). The repo already archives the Cboe term structure daily (`server/cboeVix.ts`, unread by Python — AUDIT.md §5).
- **Fit:** **VIX level or percentile is not a de-risking signal for the equity sleeve** (forward returns after high readings are higher on average, with wider dispersion); it **is** a sizing input for the short-put sleeve because outcome dispersion widens with the level (§3.6). `VIX/VIX3M > 1` (or `VIX9D > VIX`) is a clean parameter-free "stress now" flag used to halve new CSP notional and block any leverage — never to short or exit the market. VVIX scales the short-option mark-to-market budget. The 1-year vs 5-year percentile choice is untested anywhere.

### 3.4 Options-market signals (Cboe SKEW; put/call ratios; dealer gamma; implied correlation)

- **Evidence quality:** headline SKEW has no verified crash-timing power (Bilello; Liu & Faff via Bevilacqua–Tunaru); public put/call ratios are weak — the Pan–Poteshman edge needs proprietary open/close-by-customer-type data; GEX is intraday and needs open interest, which the repo archive lacks; implied correlation reacts and is not free.

### 3.5 Credit, liquidity and cross-asset (excess bond premium; HY OAS; HYG/IEF; NFCI; term spread; dollar)
- **Sources:** Gilchrist & Zakrajšek 2012 AER [ABSTRACT]; Favara–Gilchrist–Lewis–Zakrajšek 2016 FEDS Note [FULL page] and the live `ebp_csv.csv` [FULL, downloaded: monthly 1973-01 → 2026-09, latest ebp −0.39, est_prob 0.09]; FRED BAMLH0A0HYM2 page [FULL]; HYG/IEF practitioner evidence, NFCI, Welch–Goyal, Chava et al. - **Fit:** the EBP (or its `est_prob`) is the one credit variable with peer-reviewed support *and* a free maintained series: a slow monthly **downgrade-only** cap on aggregate risk for the small/mid sleeve (PQT, §2.5), never a trade trigger. HYG/IEF stays in the counterfactual log until it earns a prevention-P&L; the HY OAS is a dashboard confirmation only.

### 3.6 Premium selling under high IV (Cboe PUT/PUTY/PUTR; Israelov & Nielsen; Bondarenko; Goyal & Saretto; tastytrade deck; Christoffersen et al.)
- **Sources:** Bondarenko 2019 (Cboe) [FULL]; Cboe PUT, PUTY, PUTR factsheets as of 30 Sep 2026 [FULL]; Ennis Knupp statistics via Wikipedia [FULL page, secondary]; PUT 2008 calendar return [SEARCH-ONLY]; Israelov & Nielsen "Covered Calls Uncovered" and "Still Not Cheap" (AQR) [FULL]; AQR "Understanding the VRP" 2018 [ABSTRACT, PDFs 404]; Malkiel–Rinaudo–Saha [SEARCH-ONLY]; Goyal & Saretto 2009 JFE (2008 draft) [FULL]; tastytrade India 2020 deck [FULL, vendor]; Cboe CNDR factsheet [UNVERIFIED, 403]; Christoffersen–Goyenko–Jacobs–Karoui 2018 RFS [ABSTRACT]; Muravyev & Pearson 2020 [SEARCH-ONLY]; management-rule "studies" (50%, 21 DTE, stops) [SEARCH-ONLY, none verifiable].
- **Evidence quality:** strong as an index-level record (PUT: Jun 1986–Dec 2018 9.54%/yr vs S&P 9.80%, SD 9.95% vs 14.93%, Sharpe 0.65 vs 0.49, max DD −32.7% dated Jan 2009; since 2007 7.2%/yr, Sharpe 0.52; PUTY 2% OTM since 1986 6.9%/yr, Sharpe 0.53, max DD −28.9%; **PUTR Russell 2000 put-writing since Aug 2018: 5.3%/yr, Sharpe 0.18, max DD −32.9%** vs Russell TR 7.5%/0.21); strong for the VRP-by-VIX-decile dispersion (Israelov & Nielsen: the 80% interval of ex-post VRP widens from 5 to 19 vol points from the lowest to the highest VIX decile; worst case realized 49% above implied); strong for the cross-sectional IV-vs-HV effect (Goyal & Saretto: decile straddle returns from −9.9% to +12.6%/month on log(HV_12m/IV_1m); the 22.5%/month long-short falls to 7.5% at quoted spreads); weak for everything vendor-sourced (tastytrade: 16-delta P/L by IVR bucket $34/$43/$62/$72 with win rates 84/77/79/77% — "IVR does not impact win rates"; 30/16/5-delta P/L $65/$36/$10, "the 5 delta seemed hardly worth it"); **none** for the 50%-profit, 21-DTE and stop-loss rules, and none for spreads vs naked puts (CNDR unopened).
- **Out-of-sample:** the PUT family is live since 2007; Goyal & Saretto is in-sample 1996– (OptionMetrics).
- **Crisis behaviour (calendar-year total returns, verified):** 2008 PUT −26.8% vs S&P −37.0% [SEARCH-ONLY], max DD −32.7% [FULL], the only negative-VRP year; 2015 PUT +6.4% (S&P +1.4%), 2016 +7.8% (+12.0%); **2018 PUT −5.9% (S&P −4.4%), PUTY −2.5%** — the two-spike year produced a loss larger than the index's; **2020 PUT +2.1% in an +18.4% year, PUTY −2.4%, PUTR −0.6%** (the March mark-to-market loss was not recovered by the premium); **2022 PUT −7.7% (S&P −18.1%), PUTY −1.5%, PUTR −6.0%** (Russell −20.4%).
- **Failure modes:** a negative-VRP year is the loss year; V-recoveries are sold away; whipsaw years (2018) lose more than the index; **small-cap put-writing has not paid like the S&P premium (PUTR Sharpe 0.18)**; far-OTM puts (5–10 delta) collect too little to pay for gap risk; defined-risk wings cost most exactly when IV is high; stops on short puts convert an IV spike-and-collapse into a realized loss (the mechanism behind the repo's <1-hour closes in BASELINE.md §6); half or more of the gross single-name edge is bid-ask (Goyal & Saretto; Christoffersen: illiquid options carry a 2.5–3.4%/day return differential).
- **Leads or reacts:** n/a (strategy benchmarks).
- **Cost / data:** the repo's chain archive has IV, delta and quotes but no open interest and only ~80 days of IV history, so **true IV rank/percentile cannot be computed yet** (≈9 more months of archive); IV-minus-HV can.
- **Fit:** (1) benchmark the CSP sleeve against PUTR, not PUT; (2) size by VIX decile *down*, not up — the worst top-decile case (realized 50% above implied) must stay inside the drawdown budget; (3) write 16–30 delta, skip ≤5; in elevated IV the 2% OTM / ≈30-delta region trades return for a materially shallower drawdown, which matters more to a compounding account than the premium difference; (4) rank candidates by `log(IV_1m / HV_252)` and filter on quoted relative spread first, assuming ≥50% of the quoted spread is paid; (5) budget the book's mark-to-market on vega × expected ΔIV scaled by VVIX; (6) treat 50%/21-DTE/stop rules and spreads-vs-naked as pre-registered experiments with kill dates (the repo's own simulation of the stop rule is in `research/csp_manager_rule_sim_2026-10-26.md`), never as research-backed defaults.

## 4. Practitioner research, machine-learning classifiers, transition logic, crisis behaviour, composites, robustness

### 4.1 Practitioner research with real evidence
- **Sources (all [FULL] unless noted):** AQR "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following" (2017), "Which Trend Is Your Friend?", "Market Timing: Sin a Little"; Man AHL "The Impact of Volatility Targeting" (2018), "The Best of Strategies for the Worst of Times" (2019), "Strategic Risk Management: COVID-19" (2020, via CXO), "Trend Following and Drawdowns" (2025); Two Sigma 2021; State Street "Decoding Market Regimes" (2025); Alpha Architect trend/RAA PDFs (HTML 403); Alpha Architect/PanAgora "Protecting the Downside of Trend" (2019); Newfound "Diversifying the What, How, and When", "Protect & Participate", "Quantifying Timing Luck", "Fragility Case Study: Dual Momentum GEM", "Ensemble Multi-Asset Momentum", "Global Growth-Trend Timing", "Option-Based Trend Following"; Philosophical Economics "Growth and Trend"; Allocate Smartly 2026 (three posts); Zakamulin via CXO; Quantpedia pages; StockCharts/Hill; Keller & Keuning PAA via CXO; ReSolve "Craftsman's Perspective" [UNVERIFIED, 403]; SSRN pages for Zakamulin 2015, Keller, Harvey [UNVERIFIED, 403 — content obtained elsewhere].
- **What they settle:** (a) AQR *Century*: trend positive in every decade since 1880 across asset classes; blend 1/3/12-month signals; a one-month signal lag "especially" hurts short lookbacks; trend "may not perform well in bear markets that occur very rapidly" (1987). (b) AQR *Sin a Little*: valuation (CAPE) timing since 1958 gives Sharpe 0.37 vs 0.37 — do not build it. (c) Man 2018: the continuous vol dial (§3.1). (d) Man 2019: 1-/3-month TSMOM "tended to perform well during equity crises" while 12-month was negative in the three most recent crises including Q4 2018 (true OOS); options hedges are costly 86% of the time. (e) Man 2020 (COVID, OOS): 1-month trend "performed well", 3-month "reasonably well", vol targeting de-risked ahead of peak vol; bonds and gold did not help. (f) Man 2025: drawdown-triggered de-allocation with slow re-allocation costs 2.2%/yr in simulation. (g) Newfound: specification luck is hundreds of bps (9- vs 10-month GEM = 43% vs 146% full-period; rebalance day alone moves Sharpe 0.6–0.9 and max DD 9.9–20.8%); ensembles over 360–500 specifications land in the top Sharpe quartile and bottom drawdown quartile; the 12-month leg was ~30% behind an options replica in March 2020; the 6–12-month GEM turned defensive only in January 2019 at −14%. (h) PanAgora: slow entry (12-month sign + 200-day high) / fast exit (100-day low) gives shallower drawdowns and shorter recoveries at the same Sharpe with higher turnover. (i) Keller & Keuning PAA: exposure = the *count* of assets in uptrend ÷ 6–12; OOS-ish 1993–2015 CAGR 10.4% vs 8.2% (60/40) at max DD −8.8%. (j) Philosophical Economics GTT: on the 10-month MA, whipsaws outnumber captured bears 3–4:1 at ≈6% each; a US unemployment-trend veto raised the win rate 24% → 39% and avoided four post-2009 whipsaws worth ≈25% cumulative — US only, mixed abroad. (k) Allocate Smartly: trading the whole book faster than monthly adds churn without return (>2%/yr drag); an aggregate of 100 strategies beats a binary risk-on/off version of the same signal (max DD −10.6% vs −13.1%); equity-curve switching is overfitting. (l) StockCharts: ≈7 daily 200-day crosses/yr, >50% lasting ≤1 week; a 1% envelope or Bollinger(200,1σ) filter cuts signals 60–85%; 12 crosses in 2022. (m) Zakamulin: timing adds +7–14% to Sharpe at best; ~80% of exit-to-reentry intervals underperform buy-and-hold; the optimal lookback is index-specific.
- **Evidence quality:** strong for AQR/Man/Zakamulin; mixed for Newfound/PanAgora/Keller/Allocate Smartly (hypothetical, gross, in-sample measurement but careful); weak for StockCharts counts.

### 4.2 Machine-learning regime classifiers: honest walk-forward results versus in-sample
- **Sources:** Bulla 2011 [FULL] (§1.2); Shu–Yu–Mulvey 2024 [FULL] (§1.3); Quantpedia/Jalšovský 2026 four-state HMM with a sealed holdout [ABSTRACT-level page]; Jayawardhane & Kumari 2026 [FULL, conference]; Goswami SSRN 7375498 [UNVERIFIED, 403]; Pomorski & Gorse 2023 arXiv [FULL]; Akioyamen et al. 2020 [ABSTRACT]; Two Sigma 2021 and SSGA 2025 [FULL, descriptive].
- **What the honest holdouts show:** the sealed-holdout composite-feature HMM (realized vol, vol term structure, drawdown, Baa–Aaa, defensive-vs-cyclical momentum) finds "something real" but **no edge over a vol-targeted twin**; RF/GB/LSTM *direction* forecasts are ≈ noise while GB *volatility* forecasts beat GARCH (Jayawardhane & Kumari); Bulla's HMM adds +18.5 bp/yr on the S&P after costs; the only strong ML result (jump model) is a persistence-penalised clustering on vol features; XGBoost stacked on JM labels gives real OOS numbers but one test period, many hyperparameters, an admitted "not fully fair" comparison and up to 11.7× turnover; Two Sigma and SSGA labels are descriptive and "not predictive".
- **Verdict:** ML belongs in monitoring and volatility forecasting, not in the gate. The repo's `ml_model_v2` blend is regime-conditional on an input that was constant live (AUDIT.md §2 P9) — that is a monitoring finding, not a reason to add a classifier.

### 4.3 Transition logic: asymmetric speed, hysteresis bands, dwell, graduated exposure, flip budgets
- **(a) Asymmetric speed — "fast out, slow in".** Supported as *speed of signal*: a fast horizon (22/65-day) or the vol dial may downgrade; the slow horizon governs full re-entry (Man 2019/2020; AQR crash caveat). **Not** supported as "wait N confirming days after a sell": no opened source tests extra re-entry delay favourably; Zakamulin (80% of out-of-market intervals underperform) and Man 2025 (−2.2%/yr) point the other way; PanAgora's slow-entry filter buys drawdown reduction, not return. Re-enter on the signal, in tranches, not after a waiting period. Evidence: mixed.
- **(b) Hysteresis bands.** Bands reliably cut the *number* of signals (BLL/STW used 1% bands; StockCharts 1% envelope cuts signals sharply); no opened source shows a statistically significant OOS return improvement from a band, and Sullivan–Timmermann–White's band-bearing best rules fail OOS significance (White p = 0.154–0.908). Use a small band for churn control and verify by ablation. Evidence: weak–mixed.
- **(c) Dwell / confirmation.** The one element with honest OOS improvement over the undamped version: Bulla's 6-day majority filter (switches −50–65%, S&P Sharpe 0.39 → 0.58 in the OOS/costed run) and Shu's jump penalty (λ 50–100 gives <1 shift/yr and survives a 10-day delay while the raw HMM at 8.5 shifts/yr does not); monthly evaluation of the slow leg (Faber; Allocate Smartly). Dwell based on the bot's own P&L is not justified (Man 2025; Robot Wealth: one bad month is noise at p = 0.12). Evidence: mixed, consistent direction.
- **(d) Graduated vs binary.** Six independent practitioner sources point the same way and none shows graduation reducing returns relative to binary (Alpha Architect 0/50/100; Newfound share-of-specifications; Keller breadth fraction; Allocate Smartly aggregate vs binary; Shu linear-in-probability ≈ 0/1; Man's continuous dial). Steps should come from a *count of independent-ish votes*, not thresholds on one signal. Evidence: mixed, consistent.
- **(e) Flip budget.** Well-designed systems change the primary state **0.5–2 times per year** (Bulla filtered ≈2; Shu JM 0.4–0.9; 25/200 and 50/200 crossovers ≈1; Faber 3–4 round trips/yr across five assets; Bollinger(200,1σ) ≈0.5). ≈7/yr is the daily-close artefact; the repo's current schedule proxy flips ≈28/yr (§5).
- **(f) Parameter sensitivity.** Single-specification conclusions flip on ±10–20% lookback changes or on the rebalance day (Newfound 9- vs 10-month; 6-1 vs 11-1 = −25% vs +136% over 1927–34; Zakamulin's index-specific optima; STW's sub-period instability; Allocate Smartly: only one of many crossover pairs "worked" → luck). Ensembles over lookbacks and tranches over days are the documented fix; thresholds must be tested as *ranges*.

### 4.5 Composite regime approaches and double counting
- Combining votes beats the single best rule only in the sense of *variance reduction of specification luck* (Newfound ensembles; Allocate Smartly aggregate) and breadth-count graduation (Keller PAA); no opened source runs a trend+vol+breadth+credit vote-count composite with an honest holdout against the best single rule; the only genuinely OOS composite episode is 2020 (trend + vol both worked; bonds and gold did not).
- How to combine without double counting (Zakamulin–Giner; Levine–Pedersen; PanAgora footnote): TSMOM and price-vs-MA on the same horizon are near-identical votes — **one vote per horizon**; breadth is a separate dimension (one vote); volatility is a separate dimension but enters as a **continuous multiplier, not a vote**, to avoid re-counting the drawdown information already in trend; credit is a downgrade-only tie-breaker pending the counterfactual log; a macro veto (GTT) is US-only, lagged, and only ever suppresses whipsaw exits.

### 4.6 Parameter robustness — what "robust" means on this evidence
- Round numbers, few parameters, ranges not points: every strong source agrees (Zakamulin; Newfound; STW; Harvey's "not very sensitive" estimator result is the one place a parameter *is* robust — the vol half-life across 10–90 days).
- The repo's current thresholds (0.94/0.95/0.98/1.05/1.15/1.40 on a VXX ratio) were set by in-sample sweeps on survivor universes (AUDIT.md §10) — the opposite of this standard.
- The ±20% test required by the program is applied in §5.4.

## 5. Our own index-level replication (pre-registered, free data)

### 5.1 Harness
Scratchpad harness over `backtest_v2.fetch_bars(sym, 7000)` (Alpaca first, Yahoo fallback; 24-hour disk cache): daily closes 2007-08-28 → 2026-10-26 for SPY, QQQ, IWM, RSP, HYG, IEF, ^VIX, ^VIX3M. A signal computed at close *t* sets the exposure applied to the return from close *t+1* to close *t+2* (`lag = 2`: trade at the next session, hold from its close), 5 bp per unit of turnover, exposure capped at 1.0, cash earns nothing. Metrics: CAGR, max drawdown, Sharpe (daily, annualised), flips per year (exposure-level changes), time in market, and the same per episode (GFC 2007-08 → 2009-06; 2015-06 → 2016-03; 2018-09 → 2018-12; 2020-01 → 2020-06; 2022; 2023-01 → 2026-10). ### 5.4 Sensitivity (±20% and structural ablations, pre-registered in the harness)
- Vol target 8% / 10% / 12% around the composite: CAGR 3.91% / 4.88% / 5.99%, max DD unchanged at −16.9% (the DD floor is set by the trend legs), Sharpe 0.54 / 0.58 / 0.63 — monotone, no ordering reversal.
- Vol half-life 10 / 20 / 40 days: CAGR 5.11% / 4.88% / 4.57%, Sharpe 0.60 / 0.58 / 0.56 — flat, consistent with Harvey et al.
- Band 0 vs 1%, filter 1/3/6/10 days, monthly vs daily upgrades, 3 vs 5 steps (32 combinations): CAGR 4.35–5.79%, Sharpe 0.53–0.67, max DD −14.1% to −20.4%. Level flips fall from 44.7/yr (no band, filter 1, monthly, 3 steps) to 3.3/yr (filter 10, daily, 3 steps); the band and the filter do not change the CAGR/DD picture, only the churn. The *monthly-upgrade* rule costs CAGR in this sample (daily re-entry 5.0–5.8% vs monthly 4.4–5.3%) because 2009, 2020 and 2023 rebounds are fast — consistent with Zakamulin's out-of-market finding.
- Execution lag 1 vs 2 days: 4.77% vs 4.88%; cost 10 bp vs 5 bp: 4.70% vs 4.88% — immaterial.
- Floors: the single biggest lever is whether the engine can go fully out. Floor 0 → 0.25 → 0.5 raises composite CAGR 4.88% → 7.84% → 8.53% (with a 20% dial) while max DD goes −16.9% → −21.6% → −27.7%.
- Conclusion: no ±20% threshold change reverses any ordering that matters, and the committed tool (`scripts/regime_backtest.py --sweep`, `research/regime_backtest_2026-10-28.md`) checks four pre-registered claims under 34 variants: (i) every *trend-gated* arm has lower CAGR than buy-and-hold with leverage capped at 1; (ii) never-fully-out arms keep 70–80% of B&H CAGR while halving max DD, fully-out arms keep 40–65%; (iii) the current schedule proxy is the worst arm on CAGR and the most active by a factor of 15–20; (iv) one prior was *wrong*: the loose vol dial (20% target ≈ the index's long-run vol) is CAGR-neutral (11.05% vs 11.05%; 11.3% in two variants) while cutting max DD from −55% to −39…−43% — the dial costs CAGR only when its target is tight (10%: −3 pp), which is why the engine's target is the index's own long-run vol, not 10%.

### 5.5 Interpretation for this repo's objective
The owner's GOAL is long-run compound growth versus buy-and-hold SPY with kill switches making aggression survivable (CLAUDE.md priority 3), and the live drawdown halts sit at −10% (Node), −18% (Python) and −20% (frozen kill switch). On the index-level evidence:
1. **A regime layer cannot be expected to add CAGR here** — every trend-gated arm trails B&H because leverage is capped, and the loose vol dial is at best CAGR-neutral. Its honest product is a shallower drawdown path and a lower volatility of outcomes, which is what keeps the kill switches from firing. Any plan that promises the regime engine will *raise* returns is contradicted by this data and by Zakamulin, Cederburg and Nystrup.
2. **The current machinery is doing the opposite of what a regime layer is for**: it gives up more than half of B&H's CAGR (4.43% vs 11.05%) for a max DD (−20%) that a monthly Faber rule beats at nearly twice the CAGR, and it flips ~28 times a year. Its damage is to compounding, not to risk.
3. **Never fully out.** Floors of 0.5 on the trend legs dominate floor 0 on CAGR at a drawdown (−26% to −28%) that sits inside the owner's stated tolerance and *outside* the live halt levels — the halt levels are a separate problem for FINDINGS.md (they would have fired in 2008, 2020 and 2022 under every arm, including the composite).
4. **The vol dial earns its place only as a loose governor** (15–20% target): at 10% it costs 3 pp of CAGR for 5 pp of DD; at 20% it costs 0.3 pp for 9 pp of DD. This matches Bongaerts (act only in the extreme quintile) better than Moreira–Muir.
5. **Small caps (IWM) are worse under every arm** — the best gated IWM CAGR is 6.2% vs 8.3% B&H with DD −29% to −35%. The trend/vol layer protects the small-cap sleeve's drawdown but will not make it compound faster; the sleeve's return has to come from selection, which is the subject of §2.2 and AUDIT.md §6.
6. **Churn control (band, filter) is free**: it cuts flips by an order of magnitude at no CAGR cost — adopt it; it is not where the money is.

## 7. What the strongest evidence supports — and what not to build

**Supported (verified, OOS or long-sample, cost-aware):**
1. Regimes are volatility regimes; a regime layer is a drawdown tool, not an alpha source (§1.6, §3.1, §5).
2. One slow index trend leg (10–12 months, ensembled, monthly, tranched) plus one continuous realized-vol dial (cap 1, loose target, extreme-state action) is the whole exposure engine the evidence justifies (§2.1, §3.1, §4.5, §5).
3. Fast legs (22/65-day trend, the vol dial) may *downgrade* at any time; the slow leg governs full re-entry; re-entry happens on the signal in tranches, never after an arbitrary waiting period (§4.3a).
4. Churn control is mandatory and free: 6-day majority filter, small band, monthly slow-leg cadence, a flip budget of 0.5–2 primary changes per year with an alarm above 6 (§4.3c–e, §5.4).
5. Graduated exposure from a count of independent votes beats binary; never fully out on the equity sleeve (§4.3d, §5.5).
6. The stock-selection sleeve is conditioned, not throttled: no dip-buys below the gating index's 200-day; prefer names ≥75% of their 52-week high; suppress momentum longs in the panic state; relative strength vs the gating index (§2.2, §2.4).
7. Small/mid caps get their own gate on IWM/RSP with the same functional form and a monthly credit cap from the Fed EBP (§2.5, §3.5).
8. For the options sleeve: VIX decile and VVIX size the book *down*; VIX/VIX3M > 1 halves new notional; negative VRP blocks new short vol; 16–30 delta; IV-minus-HV ranking after a spread filter; benchmark against PUTR; management rules are experiments (§3.2, §3.3, §3.6).
9. Validation is ablation-by-element with ±20% ranges, regime-conditioned reporting, the best sub-period removed, and discounting by the number of variants tried (§4.6, §5.4).

**Do not build (the evidence is absent, negative, or the opposite sign):**
1. A supervised ML or HMM classifier as the trading switch (§4.2).
2. Fixed VIX-level exit rules for equities ("VIX > 25 → sell") (§3.3, §4.4).
3. Valuation (CAPE) timing (§4.1b).
4. Extra re-entry delays ("N confirming days after a sell") beyond the signal's own filter (§4.3a).
5. Dwell or de-allocation keyed to the bot's own drawdown or equity curve (§4.1f, §4.3c).
6. Re-evaluating the slow regime more often than monthly, or trading the whole book daily on it (§4.1k).
7. Hysteresis bands as a return enhancer (§4.3b).
8. Counting TSMOM and MA-cross on the same horizon as two votes (§4.5).
9. A single lookback or a single rebalance day for anything (§4.3f).
10. Expecting trend alone to protect against fast crashes, or vol targeting to add alpha (§2.1, §3.1).
11. Foreign-macro GTT-style filters (§4.1j).
12. Descriptive regime labels (GMM/t-mixture) as triggers (§1.5).
13. Options hedges as the whipsaw fix under this repo's cost constraints (§4.1d).
14. SKEW, public put/call, GEX, implied correlation, Hindenburg Omen, A/D divergences, breadth as a sell trigger (§2.3, §3.4).
15. A VIX percentile or term-structure *equity* de-risk trigger (§3.3) — the repo's current VXX-ratio classifier is exactly this, and §5 measures what it costs.
