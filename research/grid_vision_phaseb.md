**In-scope clean US labels available NOW: 1408 towers, 6 substations.**

- **SUBSTATION UNDERREPRESENTATION — CONFIRMED, and worse than "modest":**
  6 substation polygons across all 74 US images (~0.4% of in-scope US
  labels). The charter's flag is real and quantified. Six SS is not a
  trainable substation class on its own.
- **US-NAIP-domain gap — CONFIRMED:** ETDII US is USGS ortho, 2 regions
  (desert AZ + irrigated-plains KS), ~2016-18 vintage. No Northeast
  canopy, no NAIP radiometry/seams. Non-US (NZ) dominates the deposit's
  image count and is a different domain entirely.
- Honesty on OT: "Other Tower" is the dataset's OWN uncertainty flag
  (Documentation.pdf) — the parser keeps OT as `tower_uncertain`
  (`in_scope=False`), never folded into the 1408 clean towers.

**Consequence for the first fine-tune:** it is a **TOWER detector first**
(1408 clean US + OSM-over-NAIP weak-label tower chips). Substations get a
SEPARATE, later track once the count is bootstrapped from (a) Duke-US
zips (Hartford/Clyde/Wilmington SS polygons, not yet pulled) and (b)
OSM-seeded substation chips confirmed by the charter's amendment-1
self-bootstrapping gate (only high-confidence OSM-corroborated detections
enter the set). Reporting one blended tower+substation number would hide
the substation weakness — refused, per the per-class honesty rule.

## The chip-INDEX approach (no pixels moved until inference)

`gridvision_chips.py` turns OSM `power=tower/substation` seeds inside a
corridor bbox into a list of chip FOOTPRINTS, each carrying the exact
STAC query `gridvision_naip_stac.py` will POST downstream. It emits
`{osm_id, cls, provenance:"osm-weak-label", lat, lon, chip_bbox[W,S,E,N],
gsd_m, chip_px, stac_query}` — the stored `chip_bbox` is byte-identical
to the query's bbox (test-pinned, no precision drift). No NAIP pixels are
fetched here; streaming COG windows from MPC is the documented downstream
step (`gridvision_naip_stac.search()` → `sign_href()` → range-read).

- Chip geometry: 512 px × GSD. @0.6 m → 307.2 m edge (verified in tests).
- Corridor-restricted per the charter: only seeds inside the bbox window
  chip; the default `TX_PILOT_BBOX` is a small Austin-area window
  (verified to return 2022 TX NAIP @0.6 m from MPC), NOT the whole state.
- OSM-as-weak-label honesty is baked into `meta.provenance` and the
  script header: RECALL only, tower nodes under-mapped vs lines, meters
  of positional offset (buffer-match, never point-IoU), OSM-vs-NAIP date
  mismatch (the NAIP capture date rides every chip so the mismatch is
  visible).
- **Prerequisite gap (honest):** `scripts/build_power_tiles.sh`'s osmium
  filter omits `power=tower` (it keeps lines + substations only). Tower
  seeds need either `n/power=tower` added to that filter OR the documented
  Overpass query (`parse_overpass` handles that response shape). Until
  then the chip builder yields substation seeds only from the existing
  extract.

## PRIOR for the first fine-tune (stated BEFORE training, per REASONING #10)

- **Expectation:** a Faster R-CNN / modern-YOLO detector fine-tuned from
  COCO weights on the 1408 US ETDII towers (downsampled to 0.6 m to match
  NAIP) + OSM-over-NAIP weak-label tower chips will reach **tower AP50 in
  the 0.55-0.75 band at 0.6 m** — BELOW the 0.73 @0.3 m bar from four
  independent groups (GridTracer/PI-Detection/RetinaNet/YOLO), because
  we train and infer at 2× coarser GSD and on only 2 US regions. Prior:
  **moderate.** Substation AP will be **near-useless at first** (6 SS
  labels) — expected and pre-stated, not a surprise to explain away.
- **Ladder path:** this is DATA→SIGNAL prep for gate-1 of the detector.
  Gate-1 eval = the two-layer benchmark (research 2.10): Duke-US static
  PR curves at simulated 0.3/0.6/1.0 m GSD, THEN OSM-corridor recall on
  current NAIP with a human-sampled precision pass. OSM measures RECALL
  only; precision claims require the human sample. No per-state accuracy
  number is published until it states which it is.
- **Discount:** any GSD/architecture sweep discounts the winner by the
  number of variants tried; out-of-sample (a held-out region) confirms
  before belief.

## Proposed first RunPod job spec (GPU — the part I did NOT run)

Training is mandatory-GPU (Item 1: no released weights exist). The gate
is `scripts/runpod_budget.py` (finite `max_hours` required):

- **Workload:** `grid-detector-v0` — fine-tune Detectron2 Faster R-CNN
  R101-FPN (PI-Detection MIT starter) OR a modern YOLO, tower class
  primary, on the ETDII US chips + OSM-over-NAIP weak chips.
- **GPU:** RTX 4090 (RunPod community ~$0.34/hr; on-demand ~$0.69/hr) or
  L4 ($0.39/hr). Either fits — 30-60 M-param detector, 2-5k chips.
- **max_hours = 4** (CONCRETE CAP). Covers the research §4.6 estimate
  (1-3 h to fine-tune on a 4090) plus margin. `authorize_job` requires a
  finite positive cap; 4 h passes.
- **Est cost:** worst-case 4 × $0.34 = **$1.36** (4090 community) to
  4 × $0.69 = **$2.76** (on-demand); typical actual $1-3/run. Against the
  $50 balance this leaves ≥ $47.2 — clears the $5 floor buffer easily.
- **Budget-gate procedure (mandatory):** the launching session calls
  `runpod_budget.authorize_job(rows, "RTX4090", 0.34, 4, workload=
  "grid-detector-v0")`, launches ONLY if `authorized`, and passes
  `max_runtime_seconds = 14400` to RunPod as the hard auto-terminate cap.
  Ledger the open/close rows.
- **Weights hygiene:** Detectron2 COCO starting weights are CC-BY-SA 3.0 →
  the fine-tuned weights stay INTERNAL (share-alike inheritance);
  selling detections/outputs is fine (research 1.4 / 3.5).
